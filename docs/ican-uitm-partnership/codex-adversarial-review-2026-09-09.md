# Summary

The deck is not ready for external presentation. The supplied renders are stale relative to the HTML, slide 5 contradicts itself about how much of the workflow already exists, and the deck presents numerous safeguarding, data-protection and operational controls as current facts even though they are not in the approved fact set. It also promises access to live education data, publishable results, placements, income and potentially paid roles without defining consent, ethics review, intellectual property, costs, liability or success criteria. For a first meeting originating from a cold email, asking for three pilots, faculty coordination and an agreement draft within thirty days is overreaching and inconsistent with the stated “choose the order” narrative.

# Visual issues per slide

## Slide 1

- The two match cards read as real UiTM cases; neither has an in-card concept or fictional label.
- The front card obscures a large portion of the Nakngaji card.
- Empty gradient avatar circles look like image placeholders.
- The footer disclaimer is too small and distant to correct the impression made by the cards.

## Slide 2

- The left photograph crops the tutor’s head at the upper edge.
- Secondary labels inside the shared-engine rows are small for projection.
- The third card is text-heavy relative to the photographic cards.

## Slide 3

- The map consumes a large area without locations, coverage markers or usable information.
- “Where we operate” visually contradicts the caveat that the map is not a coverage chart.
- Several qualification lines are too small to carry important evidence distinctions in a meeting room.

## Slide 4

- “Student and alumni community, through ICAN” is visually presented as a BJKK unit when it is not.
- The source strip is cramped and uses inconsistent default hyperlink styling.
- The three-row mapping omits the attending Environmental Sustainability Unit despite the headline referring to the division’s structure.

## Slide 5

- No clipping is visible in the supplied render.
- The visual system clearly marks two new steps, making the “four of five” contradiction especially obvious.
- The arrows sit high between cards and read more as card decoration than a centred process connector.

## Slide 6

- Large blank regions separate the upper descriptions from the bottom “We need from UiTM” rows.
- The three “Suggested UiTM home” treatments vary substantially in width and visual density.
- The omitted environmental unit is conspicuous given the audience.

## Slide 7

- The application looks operational; the only concept disclaimer is in the small footer.
- Several secondary field annotations are too small for projected viewing.
- “Sifututor tutor application” is visually awkward and redundant.

## Slide 8

- This is the densest slide; faculty-fit and deliverable copy is small for a room presentation.
- The right rail has a large dead zone before its bottom note.
- Gradient circles beside supervisor roles resemble missing profile images.
- The status and disclaimer chips at the bottom are visually cramped.

## Slide 9

- The long headline and wrapped highlight create fragmented emphasis.
- Sources are detached from the precedent cards and are too small.
- The pale blue “Our proposal” card can be mistaken for a settled offer despite unresolved data and publication terms.

## Slide 10

- The slide is overloaded with fifteen control claims plus two regulatory pills.
- The regulatory pills use small text and span long horizontal distances.
- Default blue source links compete with the main content.
- The supplied render does not show overlap, but projection readability is weak.

## Slide 11

- The left side is densely packed while both right-side cards contain excessive unused space.
- Empty checkboxes can imply that agreement is expected during the meeting rather than merely discussed.
- The headline says “a first pilot,” while the body visually asks for one pilot per track.

## Slide 12

- The supplied image is stale: all three “Who:” paragraphs in the HTML are missing.
- The current HTML therefore has not been visually validated for fit or overflow.
- The first two timeline segments are blue and the last is grey, which can imply that weeks 1–3 are already complete.
- The right-hand photograph is cropped too tightly.
- Repeated imagery uses space that would be more valuable for costs, decision gates or pilot conditions.
