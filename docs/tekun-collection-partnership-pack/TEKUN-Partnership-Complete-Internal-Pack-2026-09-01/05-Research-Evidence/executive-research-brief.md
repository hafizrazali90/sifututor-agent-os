# Executive Research Brief

**TEKUN Corporation collection partnership - research findings**
**Research date:** 31 August 2026 · **Audience:** Hafiz, then Codex
**Read time:** about 10 minutes. Nothing technical is assumed.

---

## 1. The short version

TEKUN Corporation Sdn Bhd is a real, established collection business - not a
prospect that needs to be persuaded that collection matters. It has run a debt
collection call centre since September 2015, states that 30 organisations have
appointed it as their collection agent, and won at least two new appointments
during 2026, one witnessed by a government minister.

Three findings should shape everything that follows.

**First, this is a modernisation story, not a rescue story.** There is no public
evidence that TEKUN Corporation is failing at collection, and no public evidence
that it uses an outside collection company at all. Any proposal built on
"replacing your provider" or "you lack collection capability" would be both
unsupported and insulting.

**Second, Malaysia has just started regulating this industry, and regulatory
readiness is time-sensitive.** For an existing affected operator, section 135(1)
requires an application within six months from 1 June 2026. Whether the regime
applies to each TEKUN Corporation portfolio, and which authorisation path or
application status applies to TEKUN Corporation, is not publicly known. This is
an important neutral discovery topic for its compliance team.

**Third, the new rules are unusually specific, and they are effectively a
product specification.** Contact only between 8am and 9pm. No more than three
calls a week or twelve a month. A written seven-day notice before recovery
action, containing six named items. Verify identity before discussing a debt.
Never discuss it with a spouse. Never let an agent take money. Stop immediately
when someone settles. A platform that enforces these controls and preserves
auditable evidence can turn regulatory obligations into a consistent operating
capability. This is the strongest evidence-backed commercial argument available
to us.

## 4. The relationship distinction that must not be blurred

TEKUN Corporation publicly names around 30 organisations. **Those organisations
hire TEKUN Corporation.** They are its customers. MOCCIS, ANGKASA, Koperasi TNB,
PUNB, UDA Holdings and the rest are the demand side.

That is a completely different thing from any company TEKUN Corporation might
hire to do collection work for it. We searched hard for that second layer and
found nothing public.

Getting this backwards - describing a client as a vendor - would be the fastest
way to demonstrate we had not done the work.

## 5. Implications for the proposal

### 5.1 The framing that the evidence supports

> **Modernise, integrate, scale and continuously improve TEKUN Corporation's
> established collection and credit-recovery business.**

Not replace. Not rescue. Not "you have no capability". Modernise and scale
something real that is visibly growing.

### 5.2 Where the strongest argument actually lies

Not in generic efficiency claims, but in three specific places:

1. **Provable conduct compliance.** The new rules are precise and testable, and
   an authorised entity stays accountable for whatever its representatives do.
   A platform that enforces contact windows, frequency caps, notice periods,
   identity verification and cessation triggers - and can prove it did - turns a
   regulatory burden into an operational asset.

2. **Multi-principal capacity.** TEKUN Corporation added two principals in 2026
   and had a minister attend one signing. Its constraint is plausibly the
   ability to take on more principals safely, not desire. A partner who makes
   safe scaling possible participates in TEKUN Corporation's own growth.

3. **Evidence for its own clients.** Around 30 principals each need reporting
   they can trust. Reporting quality is a client-retention asset for TEKUN
   Corporation, and it is something a platform delivers directly.

**And the regulatory perimeter is still widening.** Three of TEKUN Corporation's
named clients are motor dealers. If the portfolios assigned by those clients
involve hire purchase or credit sales, the future regulatory transfer may become
relevant. The assigned products are not publicly disclosed, and no Phase 2 date
has been published. Regulatory change should therefore be treated as an ongoing
operating consideration rather than a one-off project.

### 5.3 What the proposal must never claim

- that TEKUN already uses our system;
- that any named company is TEKUN's incumbent, or that it is underperforming;
- any commission rate, portfolio size or performance figure as TEKUN's;
- that TEKUN Nasional's audit findings describe TEKUN Corporation;
- that the solution is compliant with anything;
- that profit will increase;
- ISO/IEC 27001 certification - the ISO source was inaccessible and no
  certificate was seen.

### 5.4 Recommended commercial and delivery direction

- **Start with a bounded pilot** on one principal or one arrears segment. It
  limits risk to TEKUN Corporation's live client relationships, it avoids
  colliding with the registration deadline, and - critically - **it produces the
  baseline that any performance-based commercial model requires.** No current
  TEKUN Corporation performance baseline was available in the public sources
  reviewed.
- **Favour a hybrid fixed-plus-performance structure.** A pure success fee
  creates exactly the pressures the new conduct rules exist to prevent, and the
  regulator explicitly requires that collection remuneration promote fair
  outcomes. A fixed floor funds hardship handling, complaints and quality; the
  performance element rewards results above an agreed baseline.
- **Propose no price yet.** Ten pieces of information are missing, and every one
  of them changes the number.

## 7. Legal and commercial cautions

### Legal

- **Whether the Consumer Credit Act applies to any given TEKUN portfolio is
  genuinely unresolved.** Schedule 1 excludes credit provided by entities that
  receive government funding to run micro-financing schemes, which appears to
  describe TEKUN Nasional closely. But TEKUN Corporation collects for roughly 30
  principals, including co-operatives and private motor dealers, whose credit is
  not covered by that exclusion. **This requires TEKUN Corporation's compliance
  function and qualified Malaysian legal counsel.** We must not assert a
  conclusion in either direction.
- **If our role is legally structured as a data processor, statutory duties may
  apply directly to us**, including the Security Principle and applicable DPO
  requirements. The controller-processor relationship, thresholds and resulting
  obligations require confirmation from counsel and the parties' compliance
  functions.
- **Never claim compliance.** We can describe controls and map them to rules.
  Only counsel and TEKUN's compliance function can conclude compliance.

### Commercial

- **No current baseline was available to this research.** A performance-linked
  structure should not be finalised until TEKUN Corporation supplies an agreed,
  validated baseline or a pilot establishes one.
- **Define every paid-against metric in writing** - numerator, denominator,
  population, period, exclusions. "Collection rate" alone means at least four
  different things.
- **Commission-heavy models carry real conduct risk** and must be tested against
  the regulator's fair-outcomes requirement before they are proposed.
- **Recovery costs can never be passed to the customer.** Any fee design that
  assumes otherwise is not merely unattractive; it is prohibited.
- **Commit to transition readiness from day one.** A government-linked
  subsidiary facing procurement, audit and now regulatory scrutiny will discount
  a partner it cannot leave. Being genuinely easy to leave is the strongest trust
  signal we have - and section 2 of the durability file explains why it costs us
  nothing real.
