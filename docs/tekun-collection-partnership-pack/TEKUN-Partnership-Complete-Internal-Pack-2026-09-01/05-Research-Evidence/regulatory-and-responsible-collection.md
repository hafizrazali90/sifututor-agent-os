**Position stated as at 31 August 2026.** Covers research areas F, G and H.

> **This is not legal advice.** Every question of whether a rule applies to TEKUN
> Corporation's specific portfolios requires confirmation with TEKUN
> Corporation's compliance function and qualified Malaysian legal counsel.

## 1. The headline, in plain language

Malaysia now regulates debt collection agencies for the first time. A new
regulator, the **Suruhanjaya Kredit Pengguna (SKP)** - Consumer Credit Commission
can register them, examine them, investigate them and prosecute them.

Three things follow, and they are all time-critical:

1. **Registration is now required** for a debt collection business that falls
   within the Act. Section 57 creates an offence for carrying on that applicable
   business unregistered, subject to the Act's scope, transition and enforcement
   provisions.
2. **Regulatory readiness is time-sensitive.** For an existing affected operator,
   section 135(1) requires an application within six months from 1 June 2026.
   Exact applicability, deadline and application status require confirmation.
3. **Detailed conduct rules already bind day-to-day collection work** - contact
   hours, call frequency caps, notice periods, identity verification, who may
   receive money, and how records must be kept. These are not principles. They
   are specific and testable, and they translate almost line-by-line into
   platform requirements.

**Statutory basis for the transition period.** Section 135(1) of Act 873 requires

breach."* SKP also referred to requirements that *"will come into force at the
end of the year."*

> **Contradiction resolved.** One secondary summary described the transition as
> six months *ending* 1 June 2026. That is inconsistent with both the MOF and SKP
> releases and with section 135(1) of the Act itself. The correct reading is that
> the six months **begins** 1 June 2026. Primary sources win.

### The credit-consumer threshold, as the regulator explains it

SKP's authorisation guidance states that a micro or small enterprise is a credit
consumer where it obtains credit **up to RM300,000**, and expressly notes that an
MSE granted a facility **exceeding** RM300,000 is **not** a credit consumer.
"Micro or small enterprise" is stated to follow the definition used by **SME
Corporation Malaysia**.

> **Relevance.** TEKUN Niaga financing is publicly stated as RM1,000-RM100,000,
> well inside the threshold. On amount alone, such borrowers sit within the MSE
> limb. Whether the Act nonetheless applies still turns on Schedule 1 - see §4.

## 3. Is debt collection regulated? Yes - and here is exactly how

### 3.1 Debt collection is a "credit service business"

**Schedule 3** of Act 873 lists the types of credit service business, the first
being **Debt collection**, defined as:

> collecting debts or financial obligations due under a credit agreement on
> behalf of a credit provider or credit service provider carrying on the business
> of impaired loan or financing acquisition - other than collections done by an
> advocate and solicitor regulated under the Legal Profession Act 1976.

Three elements matter for TEKUN:

| Element | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| "due under a **credit agreement**" | The underlying obligation must be credit. Not every receivable qualifies |
| "**on behalf of** a credit provider…" | This is third-party collection - precisely TCorp's model |
| "other than… an advocate and solicitor" | Law-firm litigation recovery sits outside this registration regime |

### 3.2 Registration requirement and statutory offence

**Schedule 4, paragraph 3(a)** places *Debt collection* among the credit service
businesses **registered by the Commission**.

**Section 57(1):** no person shall carry on such business, or hold himself out as
carrying it on, unless registered.
**Section 57(2):** contravention is an offence - fine up to **RM5 million**,
imprisonment up to **five years**, or both.

**Schedule 7** excludes section 57(1) from the section 106 administrative
monetary-penalty route. Section 57(2) creates a criminal offence. The Act also
contains enforcement and compounding provisions, so the applicable enforcement
path in a particular case must be left to the authorities and qualified counsel.

### 3.3 Registration criteria and cost

From the SKP Authorisation Standards v1.0 (issued and effective 5 June 2026):

| Requirement | Debt collection |
| --- | --- |
| Minimum shareholders' funds or total equity | **RM500,000** |
| If also authorised for another business type | The **higher** threshold applies |
| Annual registration fee | RM5,000 (<RM3m revenue) · RM12,000 (RM3m-<15m) · RM25,000 (RM15m-<50m) · RM50,000 (≥RM50m) |
| Fee due | Last day of February each year |
| Complaints data submission to SKP | Within **6 months** of the authorisation date (para 13.4) |
| Credit consumer data to a credit reporting agency | Within **12 months** of the authorisation date (para 13.6) |
| Fit and proper, directors, senior management, controllers | Sections 62-65 of the Act |
| Application route | SKP **CORE System** |

**Incidental-activity carve-out.** The Authorisation Standards explain that an
entity may run an internal collection unit for its *own* customers without
separately registering as a debt collection agency - but only if the activity
serves exclusively internal needs (not even related corporations), is secondary,
is not a significant part of the business, and the entity does **not hold itself
out as a debt collection agency or offer such services to third parties**.

> **Interpretation.** This carve-out clearly does **not** cover TCorp. TCorp
> publicly describes itself as a debt collection agency and offers the service to
> around 30 external organisations. The carve-out is the opposite of TCorp's
> model.

### 3.4 The registration status question

| Has TEKUN Corporation submitted a section 79(2) declaration instead? | **Not publicly disclosed** |

Section 76 of the Act requires publication of the names of licensed credit

## 4. Does the Act actually apply to TEKUN's portfolios? The critical nuance

This is the single most important - and most easily mishandled - legal question
in the entire pack. It is genuinely unresolved on public evidence.

### 4.1 The Act applies only where a "credit consumer" is involved

**Section 2(1):** the Act applies to any person carrying on a credit business or
credit service business **involving a credit consumer**, except for credit
provided by a person set out in Schedule 1.

**Section 5(1)** defines a credit consumer as:

- an individual obtaining credit wholly or predominantly for personal, domestic
  or household purposes;
- a **micro or small enterprise** obtaining credit not exceeding a prescribed
  amount - prescribed as **RM300,000** by P.U. (A) 209/2026;
- any other person or class specified by the Commission;
- an individual acting as a **social guarantor**, not for profit.

> TEKUN Niaga financing is publicly stated as RM1,000-RM100,000. On amount alone,
> such borrowers would fall **within** the MSE limb of "credit consumer".

### 4.2 But Schedule 1 excludes certain credit entirely

**Schedule 1** lists credit *not subject to the application of the Act*,
including credit provided by:

- **(b)** the Government, State Government, local authority, **statutory body**,
  government-linked company or corporation, or state corporation; and
- **(c)** **any entity receiving funding from the Government or State Government
  to administer or implement micro-financing schemes.**

**The relevant facts.** The Auditor-General's report records that TEKUN Nasional
is a company limited by guarantee established in 1998, sits under KUSKOP, and
received **RM395 million in government programme funding in 2021** to run
micro-financing schemes.

> **Interpretation, flagged as requiring legal confirmation.** Limb (c) appears
> to fit TEKUN Nasional closely: an entity receiving government funding to
> administer or implement micro-financing schemes. If Schedule 1 applies to TEKUN
> Nasional's credit, then that credit is outside the Act's application, and
> collection *of that specific portfolio* may sit outside the registration
> requirement.
>
> **This does not settle anything**, because:
> - TCorp collects for roughly 30 principals, not only TEKUN Nasional;
> - co-operative and private motor-dealer portfolios are different credit
>   entirely and are not covered by Schedule 1 limb (b) or (c);
> - Schedule 3 defines debt collection as collecting on behalf of a **credit
>   provider**, and section 4 defines "credit provider" narrowly by reference to
>   licensing or other written law - so whether a given principal is a "credit
>   provider" for the Act's purposes is itself a legal question;
> - one portfolio being out of scope does not put the *business* out of scope.

**Requires confirmation with TEKUN Corporation's compliance function and
qualified Malaysian legal counsel.**

### 4.3 The co-operative dimension

**Schedule 5** disapplies Part V (licensing and registration) to, among others,
**co-operative societies registered under subsection 7(1) of the Co-operative
Societies Act 1993**.

A large share of TCorp's named clients are co-operatives - MOCCIS, ANGKASA,
Koperasi UTM, KOPETRO, Koperasi TNB, Koperasi Permodalan FELDA and others.

> **Interpretation, requiring legal confirmation.** Schedule 5 exempts the
> **co-operative itself** from needing a licence or registration. On its face it
> does not say that a separate company collecting on a co-operative's behalf is
> exempt from registering as a debt collection agency. Those are different
> questions and should not be conflated.

Note also **section 34(1)**: the Malaysia Co-operative Societies Commission (SKM)
is itself one of the Regulatory and Supervisory Authorities under the Act,
alongside SKP, Bank Negara Malaysia, the Securities Commission, the Ministry of
Domestic Trade and Cost of Living (KPDN) and the Ministry of Housing and Local
Government (KPKT). Co-operative portfolios may therefore involve SKM as well.

### 4.4 The section 79(2) declaration route

**Section 79(2):** a person carrying on a credit business or credit service
business under Schedule 4 paragraph 1 or 3 **that does not involve a credit
consumer** must submit a declaration of that fact to the Commission.

Authorisation Standards paragraph 8.3 requires that declaration to state that the
entity does not serve credit consumers, that it will re-declare **annually**, and
that it will seek prior authorisation before serving any credit consumer. It is
submitted through the CORE System.

> **Practical meaning.** There is no "do nothing" option. An entity in TCorp's
> position either **registers** or **declares**. Both are affirmative acts with
> SKP, and both have deadlines.

## 5. Conduct rules that bind collection work

Source: **SKP Conduct Standards v1.0 (SKP-S-CS-2026-01)**, issued and effective
5 June 2026, made under section 123 of the Act. Appendix I of the Standards
confirms that **every paragraph of Chapter 12 (Fair debt collection) and Chapter
14 (Complaints handling) applies to a debt collection agency (DCA).**

**Paragraph 2.2 is the governance keystone:** where an authorised entity enters
into any arrangement with a representative, **the authorised entity remains
accountable for that representative's actions and conduct.** Section 114 of the
Act reinforces this - an offence committed by an employee, officer, director,
partner, controller or **agent** acting on a person's behalf is deemed committed
by that person, with the same penalty.

> **What this means for our proposed role.** If our team performs or coordinates
> collection activity for TCorp, TCorp does not transfer its accountability to
> us. It keeps it. Every control we build must therefore be designed to give
> TCorp **provable oversight**, not merely to make our own work efficient.

### 5.1 Before recovery action

| Rule | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| 12.1 | Act professionally and reasonably at all times when collecting |
| 12.2 | Past-due accounts may be managed internally **or outsourced** to external debt collectors |
| **12.3** | Where collection is outsourced, the authorised entity **must ensure the external debt collector is registered with SKP** |
| **12.4** | The **reward and remuneration system** for collection representatives must promote fair outcomes for credit consumers |
| 12.5 | Issue clear, timely repayment reminders disclosing the outstanding amount with a breakdown of interest/profit and other fees, and the missed repayment due date; clearly explain the consequences of ignoring reminders |
| **12.6** | Issue a **written recovery notice at least 7 calendar days before** recovery action begins, disclosing: outstanding amount including interest/profit and charges; missed due date; commencement date of recovery actions; extended repayment due date; name and contact details of the recovery department **or appointed external debt collector**; and any change in the appointment of external debt collectors |
| 12.7 | Make best efforts to ensure reminders and notices actually reach the consumer, including the last known address or email |
| 12.8 | Recognised non-contactable circumstances: returned mail or undeliverable email; repeated unsuccessful attempts by phone or agreed channels; no response over a reasonable period |
| **12.9** | **Retain records of all communication attempts** and demonstrate reasonable steps based on the most recent reliable contact information |

### 5.2 During recovery

| Rule | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| **12.10** | Representatives must hold clear and accurate consumer information **and** a verification document proving authorisation to act, containing: name and contact of the authorised entity; name and contact of the external party if applicable; representative name and identification number; photo identification; and **validity period of the appointment** |
| 12.11 | Consumers are encouraged to verify representative identity before engaging |
| **12.12** | Representatives must **confirm the consumer's identity before discussing the debt**. Debt information must **not** be disclosed to spouses, family members or any third party without the consumer's **explicit written consent** |
| **12.13** | All payments must be made **directly to the authorised entity**; representatives must **not accept payments** on its behalf |
| 12.14 | A receipt or statement acknowledging payment must be issued |
| **12.15(a)** | No false, misleading or inaccurate information - including misleading on the amount owed or **falsely implying legal authority** such as claiming court instructions |
| **12.15(b)** | No undue psychological pressure or fear - no threatening, foul or intimidating language; **no public shaming such as posting debts on social media**; no causing or threatening bodily harm; no property damage or forcible removal of items |
| **12.15(c)(i)** | **Contact times: 8.00 a.m. to 9.00 p.m.**, unless the consumer requests otherwise |
| **12.15(c)(ii)** | **No excessive contact - no more than 3 times per week or 12 times per month** |
| **12.15(c)(iii)** | **Workplace visits only as a last resort**, and only if: other attempts failed; the consumer is not contactable elsewhere; the consumer agreed to the visit; or the consumer is the proprietor or director of the business related to the debt |
| 12.15(d) | No threatening to disclose account details or publish non-payment; no public humiliation |
| 12.15(e) | No unlawful entry or refusing to leave the consumer's residence, workplace or business premises |
| **12.15(f)** | **No contacting or attempting recovery from anyone other than the actual credit consumer** - including family, friends, relatives, employer or employees - even to locate the consumer |
| **12.16** | Costs of recovery by externally appointed representatives **must not be passed on to the credit consumer** as debt collection charges |

### 5.3 After recovery

| Rule | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| 12.17 | Thoroughly investigate all debt-recovery complaints and take remedial action for misconduct |
| **12.18** | **Cease recovery action immediately** and update credit status promptly when the consumer has regularised the account, fully settled, or been accepted under a debt resolution plan |

### 5.4 Financial hardship (Chapter 13 and section 86)

Section 86 lets the Commission specify hardship circumstances and the form of
assistance. Conduct Standards paragraph 13.1 specifies these circumstances:

critical illness · critical injury · non-voluntary loss of income · natural
disaster · death of a breadwinner · any other reasonable cause determined by SKP.

**Section 86(5) is operationally significant:** while a hardship application is
being assessed, the credit provider's right to commence **any proceedings,
execution or other legal process** against the consumer is **suspended**.

Paragraph 13.2 requires a prominently displayed, easily reachable contact point
for hardship assistance during working hours. Paragraph 13.3 sets response
classifications (approved and accepted; approved but rejected without appeal;
approved but rejected with appeal; not approved without appeal; not approved with
appeal), with a flow chart at Appendix IV and complaint turnaround times at
Appendix V.

> **Platform consequence.** A hardship application must be able to **place a
> legal hold on an account automatically**. If the system cannot suspend
> escalation, a compliant process cannot be guaranteed.

### 5.5 Competency of representatives (Chapter 15)

| Rule | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| 15.1 | Assess representatives for academic qualifications, experience, character, integrity and commitment |
| **15.2** | Assess **before initial appointment, then annually**, or whenever information materially compromising competency emerges |
| 15.3 | Adverse track record checks: investigations or criminal proceedings; contravention of laws protecting the public against financial loss from dishonesty, incompetence or malpractice; deceitful, oppressive or improper business practices; dismissal or resignation from a position of trust over honesty or integrity concerns |
| 15.4 | Adequate training on behaviours delivering fair outcomes; keeping current; familiarity with fraud risks and end-to-end fraud investigation |
| 15.5 | Ensure representatives do not engage in prohibited business conduct; no mis-selling or misrepresentation; explain key terms and default consequences; exercise due care with consumer information |

### 5.6 Prohibited business conduct under the Act

**Section 87(1)** prohibits the conduct in **Schedule 6**; contravention carries a
fine up to **RM5 million**, imprisonment up to five years, or both, and is
excluded from the monetary-penalty route by Schedule 7.

Schedule 6 covers: misleading or deceptive conduct about credit products or
services; inducing a consumer through misleading, false or deceptive statements,
dishonest concealment or ambiguous presentation of material facts, or reckless
statements; **exerting undue pressure or influence, or using or threatening
harassment, coercion or physical force** against the consumer **or a related
third party**; and demanding payment for unsolicited products or services,
**including threatening to bring legal proceedings**.

## 6. Data protection and security

### 6.1 The legal framework and its exact commencement

| Instrument | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Personal Data Protection Act 2010 | Act 709, the principal Act |
| Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Act 2024 | **Act A1727**, gazetted 24 December 2024 |
| Commencement | **P.U. (B) 522**, dated 19 December 2024 |

P.U. (B) 522 appoints, under section 1(2) of Act A1727:

| Date | Provisions of Act A1727 |
| --- | --- |
| **1 January 2025** | sections 7, 11, 13 and 14 |
| **1 April 2025** | sections 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 10 and 12 |
| **1 June 2025** | sections 6 and 9 |

**Section 6 of A1727 is the one that inserts Division 1a** - the Data Protection
Officer and data breach notification duties. Those therefore came into force on
**1 June 2025**.

> This is gazette-level precision. Several secondary summaries describe the
> phases only loosely. Use the gazette.

### 6.2 What the amendments actually require

| New or amended provision | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| New **section 12A** (PDPA) | A **data controller** must appoint one or more Data Protection Officers accountable for compliance. Where processing is carried out by a **data processor** on the controller's behalf, **the data processor must also appoint one or more DPOs**. The controller must notify the Commissioner of the appointment. Appointing a DPO does **not** discharge the controller or processor from any duty |
| New **section 12B** (PDPA) | Where a controller has reason to believe a personal data breach has occurred, it must notify the Commissioner **as soon as practicable**. Where the breach causes or is likely to cause **significant harm** to the data subject, the controller must also notify the data subject **without unnecessary delay**. Contravention of the notification duty: fine up to **RM250,000**, imprisonment up to 2 years, or both |
| Amended **section 5** | Where processing is carried out by a data processor on a controller's behalf, **the processor must comply with the Security Principle in section 9**. Penalty raised to a fine up to **RM1 million**, imprisonment up to **3 years**, or both |
| Amended **section 9** | The Security Principle now binds **both the data controller and the data processor** |
| Terminology | "data user" is replaced by "data controller" throughout |
| Definition | "data subject" no longer includes a deceased individual |

> **Potential consequence for the proposed operating model.** Depending on the
> actual data flows and agreements, our delivery team may be a data processor,
> sub-processor or joint controller. That classification determines which
> statutory security, DPO, breach and contractual duties apply. The parties'
> compliance functions and qualified counsel must confirm the roles and any
> applicable thresholds before the operating model is finalised.

### 6.3 The 72-hour rule - and where it actually comes from

The **Act** says "as soon as practicable". The **72-hour** figure comes from the
Commissioner's guideline, not the statute:

> **Personal Data Protection Guideline: Data Breach Notification, Version 1.0,
> issued 25 February 2025** - paragraph 6.1: notification shall be made as soon
> as practicable and **no later than seventy-two (72) hours** from the occurrence
> of the personal data breach.

**"Significant harm"** is defined at paragraph 5.2 as a risk that the compromised
data may result in physical harm, financial loss, a negative effect on credit
records, or damage to or loss of property; may be misused for illegal purposes;
consists of sensitive personal data; or, combined with other information, could
enable identity fraud. Paragraph 8.1 requires notification to affected data
subjects where the breach results in or is likely to result in significant harm;
paragraph 8.2 applies the same definition but excludes the "significant scale"
criterion for that purpose.

> **Precision matters.** Never write "the PDPA requires notification within 72
> hours" without saying that the 72-hour limit is set by the Commissioner's
> guideline. Getting this wrong in a stakeholder document is exactly the kind of
> legal overstatement that damages credibility.

- Data Protection Impact Assessment Guideline (DPIA)

### 6.5 Security controls the regulator already requires

The SKP Conduct Standards Chapter 16 sets out specific security expectations for
authorised entities handling credit consumer information. These are **regulatory
requirements**, and they map straight into platform and organisational controls.

| Rule | Requirement | Belongs to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 16.1-16.2 | Protect against theft, loss, misuse, unauthorised access, modification or disclosure across the whole information lifecycle: collection, storage, use, transmission, sharing, disclosure, disposal. Investigate breaches and prevent recurrence | Both |
| 16.3 | No disclosure to third parties unless required by law/court/authority or with consumer consent | Both |
| 16.4 | Preventive **and detective** ICT controls | Platform |
| **16.5** | Consumer information on any storage medium adequately protected, including **password protection and data encryption** | Platform |
| 16.6-16.7 | Strongly deter unauthorised disclosure by representatives - awareness of disciplinary action, **CCTV in relevant areas**, restricting personal electronic devices in high-risk areas **like call centres** | Organisation |
| **16.8** | **Restrict access to web-based communication and social media platforms**, particularly end-to-end encrypted ones, for staff handling consumer information | Organisation + endpoint |
| **16.9** | Mechanisms for prompt detection of: unauthorised access; **unusual or frequent viewing** of consumer information by representatives; **unusual or suspicious downloading**; unauthorised disclosure to external parties | Platform |
| 16.10 | Such mechanisms may include key-logger software, **regular audit-trail reviews**, and random periodic sample checks | Platform + organisation |
| 16.11 | A **cyber risk framework** proportionate to size and complexity, covering governance, resilience objectives, risk tolerance, and identify/protect/detect/respond/recover for systems and data hosted on-premises **or by third-party service providers** | Organisation |
| 16.12 | Cyber resilience measures including threat identification and defined incident-handling procedures | Organisation |
| **16.13-16.14** | A **data-loss prevention** strategy: accountable data owners, data classification and inventory, **clearly identified third-party-accessible data with contractual safeguards**, and technology enforcing DLP policy with alerts on violations | Both |
| 16.15-16.16 | Physical security for paper and electronic information; restricted access to server and filing rooms; **clear-desk policy** | Organisation |

> **Paragraph 16.13(c) is the one to notice.** It expressly requires that data
> accessible by **third parties** be identified, safeguarded and covered by
> adequate contractual agreements. A managed-service arrangement of the kind
> proposed is squarely within its scope.

## 7. Responsible collection operating practice

Malaysian regulatory sources are preferred and are the binding standard. The SKP
Conduct Standards already cover most of the lifecycle, so international material
is used only where local sources are silent - and is labelled as a benchmark.

| Lifecycle stage | Malaysian requirement (binding) | Where local sources are silent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early intervention | Reminders before recovery action (12.5) | Contact-strategy design is a benchmark question |
| Ageing and delinquency stages | Not prescribed by SKP | Use portfolio-quality conventions - see the KPI file |
| Segmentation and prioritisation | Not prescribed | Benchmark; must respect ADMP guidance if automated |
| Contact strategy | 8am-9pm; ≤3 per week, ≤12 per month (12.15(c)) | Channel mix is a benchmark question |
| Omnichannel communication | Reminders and notices must reach the consumer (12.7); records retained (12.9) | Channel selection is a benchmark question |
| Identity verification | Mandatory before discussing debt (12.12) | n/a |
| Third-party disclosure | Prohibited without explicit written consent (12.12); no recovery from third parties (12.15(f)) | n/a |
| Promises to pay, broken promises, partial payments | Not prescribed by SKP | Benchmark; Dynamics 365 treats promise-to-pay and broken promise-to-pay as first-class collection statuses |

### International benchmark, clearly labelled

**CGAP, *Responsible Digital Credit: Frontier Solutions for Authorities and
Providers*, July 2025** (Izaguirre, Valenzuela, Arenaza, Meagher) frames
consumer-risk management across identification, prevention, mitigation and
resolution over the whole borrower journey, and identifies over-indebtedness,
fraud and data misuse, weak transparency and **inadequate redress mechanisms** as
core risks. For providers it points to tech-enabled authentication and fraud
detection, automated debt monitoring, and privacy-enhancing features such as
encryption and consent tools.

**This is a benchmark, not Malaysian law.** It is useful for arguing *why*
responsible design is good practice. It must never be presented as a Malaysian
legal requirement.
