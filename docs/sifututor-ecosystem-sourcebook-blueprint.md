# Blueprint — The Sifututor Ecosystem: Built to Operate, Ready to Scale

**Approved subtitle:** An end-to-end sourcebook of our systems, capabilities, operating maturity, business impact and expansion foundation.

**Artifact purpose:** Define exactly what the sourcebook will contain, how it will be structured, how claims will be verified and how business and technical readers will navigate it. This is not the sourcebook itself and is not a slide blueprint.

**Document status:** Product/document design blueprint for review.

## 1. Governing direction

### 1.1 Central story

Sifututor is an end-to-end education-service operating ecosystem. Its systems work together to attract demand, receive learning requests, identify suitable tutors, establish classes, support delivery, verify service, manage billing and payments, resolve issues, analyse performance and continuously improve the platform.

The document's proof hierarchy is:

1. **Primary proof:** complete end-to-end operating coverage for a live education service.
2. **Supporting proof:** a lean, capital-efficient team built and operates this breadth of capability.
3. **Resulting value:** service quality, operational efficiency, resilience, financial integrity, learning impact and revenue opportunity.

### 1.2 Governing roadmap strategy

> Consolidate the operating spine, then multiply its value across users, brands, products, verticals and markets.

Future products, AI and geographic expansion must be shown as leverage created by trusted shared foundations. They must not appear as unrelated feature accumulation or committed launch promises.

### 1.3 Audience

The master sourcebook serves:

- owners and senior leadership;
- internal operations, CX, finance, tutor-experience, marketing and product teams;
- technical staff and future developers;
- partners and selected stakeholders;
- presentation creators who need reliable source material but will decide their own slide narrative.

### 1.4 Explicit non-goals

The sourcebook will not:

- prescribe slide order, speaker notes or presentation titles;
- publish daily development progress, completion percentages, sprint state or temporary blockers;
- expose defects, vulnerability lists, incident payloads or exploitable operational detail;
- disclose credentials, access paths, IP addresses, database connections, production data or sensitive topology;
- publish confidential revenue, margins, salaries, bank values or detailed commission economics;
- publish brand-level shared-infrastructure cost allocations unless finance has verified the allocation method and approved the figures for the intended audience;
- blend Sifututor and Nakngaji metrics, users or product behaviour;
- present roadmap capabilities as already operational;
- promise a country, product or launch date that leadership has not committed to.

## 2. Editorial and visual system

### 2.1 Writing pattern

Every substantial chapter follows the same reader ladder:

1. **In one minute:** plain-language summary.
2. **Why it matters:** practical business meaning.
3. **How it works:** process and system explanation.
4. **Technical depth:** architecture, data, integrations and controls.
5. **Evidence:** verified claims and proof references.
6. **What it enables:** business impact and strategic leverage.

Terms are defined before use. Technical language follows, rather than replaces, the business explanation.

### 2.2 Visual direction

- Premium corporate editorial foundation.
- Clean hierarchy, generous spacing and confident typography.
- Controlled bold-innovation treatment for AI, analytics, automation and Agent OS.
- Education and human-service context remains visible.
- Avoid excessive futuristic styling, dense software-dashboard screenshots and decorative diagrams with no explanatory value.

### 2.3 Positive truth standard

The document tells a progress and capability story without becoming misleading.

Use:

> Foundation established → Capability expanded → Business value created → Future leverage enabled

Avoid:

- weakness and gap lists;
- self-awarded maturity scores;
- unsupported superlatives such as “world class” or “enterprise grade”;
- volatile implementation labels;
- ambiguous scale claims;
- language that turns an aspiration into a current capability.

Truth safeguard:

> A positive claim must not omit material context that would change a reasonable reader's interpretation of its significance.

This safeguard does not reintroduce weakness lists. It ensures that progress remains credible rather than selectively edited.

## 5. Evidence and claim-verification plan

### 5.1 Claim classes

1. **Historical:** incorporation, operating start, May 2025 go-live and major milestones.
2. **Scale:** registered, verified, active, served and cumulative figures.
3. **Capability:** what a system demonstrably does.
4. **Architecture:** authority, integration and infrastructure statements.
5. **Reliability:** monitoring, backup, recovery and continuity proof.
6. **Business impact:** causal explanation or verified operational result.
7. **Roadmap:** approved direction, not current capability.
8. **External obligation:** current official legal, regulatory or standards source.

### 5.2 Evidence hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. verified internal production aggregates or controlled evidence;
2. current authoritative code, architecture and operational documentation;
3. approved company records and leadership confirmation;
4. official public company channels;
5. government, standards body or primary research sources;
6. reputable secondary sources only when primary evidence is unavailable.

### 5.3 Metric truth rules

- Distinguish registered, verified, active, served and cumulative.
- Never mix Sifututor and Nakngaji metrics.
- Resolve conflicting public and internal figures before publication.
- Add “as of” dates to numerical operating claims.
- Use qualitative language when a number cannot be verified safely.
- Do not use app-store download counts as active-user counts.
- Do not infer headcount from conflicting public directories; use an internally verified range or qualitative “lean team.”
- Do not omit context that materially changes what a positive number or capability claim means.

### 5.4 Evidence-reference rule

- Appendix E owns the claim text, evidence source, verification date, evidence owner, expiry date and shareability class.
- Narrative chapters may paraphrase an approved claim for readability but must cite its claim ID.
- System profiles, maturity tables, milestone tables, roadmap tables and the KPI dictionary must contain claim-ID references rather than duplicate evidence descriptions.
- When a claim changes, update Appendix E first and then review every sourcebook location listed on that claim row.

### 5.5 Shareability classes

- **Public-safe:** already public and verified.
- **Stakeholder-safe:** suitable for selected external sharing without operational exposure.
- **Internal:** useful to teams but not intended for external circulation.
- **Controlled/confidential:** evidence may validate a claim but must not appear in the sourcebook body.

The sourcebook is ready only when:

- achieved capability, current direction and future optionality are visibly distinct;

- Sifututor and Nakngaji remain distinct brands and metrics;
- retired systems are excluded;
- no confidential financial, personal, credential or infrastructure detail is exposed;
- the document can support multiple presentation narratives without prescribing slides;
- ownership and the next six-month review date are visible.
