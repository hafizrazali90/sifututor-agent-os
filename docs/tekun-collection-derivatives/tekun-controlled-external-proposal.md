## 1. Executive summary

TEKUN Corporation already has collection experience, client relationships and an established operating identity. **TCorp states** that its debt-collection customer call centre has operated since 2015, that it is registered with the Ministry of Finance under field code 221105 and that approximately 30 organisations have appointed it. Its official 2026 announcements also describe continuing collection or recovery appointments. The opportunity is therefore not to start a collection business from zero. It is to connect and strengthen an existing capability.

We propose a partnership with two integrated components:

1. a **dedicated collection platform owned and governed by TEKUN Corporation**, designed, built, operated, maintained and continuously improved by our team; and
2. an **authorised managed collection operation**, performed or coordinated by our team within confirmed policy, contractual and legal boundaries.

Together, these components create one operating spine across portfolio intake, segmentation, assignments, controlled contact, promises, protected cases, payment evidence, reconciliation, cessation, reporting and improvement. TEKUN Corporation retains governance, policy and consequential authority. Our team supplies platform engineering, service operations, evidence, support and improvement.

The recommended decision is deliberately bounded: authorise discovery and pilot co-design, then commit to implementation only when authority, evidence, customer-treatment, technical and commercial gates are satisfied.

### What this creates

- clearer case ownership and authorised next actions;
- consistent and provable customer treatment;
- payment outcomes tied to reconciled evidence rather than unverified reports;
- segregated reporting for each principal and an enterprise view for TEKUN Corporation;
- a repeatable way to onboard additional portfolios and appointing organisations;
- platform health, service management and improvement disciplines around daily collection work.

## 2. The opportunity: strengthen an established operator

The proposal begins with a respectful fact: TEKUN Corporation is already active in credit management and recovery. TCorp's own public materials state corporate registration in 2014 and establishment of its debt-collection customer call centre in 2015; its dated announcements describe new collection or recovery activity in 2026.

This history matters because the partnership should preserve TEKUN Corporation's identity, principal relationships and operating knowledge. The platform and managed service are intended to make that capability easier to govern, measure and scale.

Public information does not disclose TEKUN Corporation's current platform, detailed performance baseline, portfolio volumes, commission model or any current third-party arrangement. This proposal makes no assumption about those matters. They form part of discovery.

## 3. Why now

Malaysia's consumer-credit environment places greater emphasis on authorisation, conduct, evidence and complaints handling. Published expectations translate directly into operating requirements: controlled contact, identity verification, notices, non-disclosure, payment handling, hardship and complaint pathways, representative competency and information security.

Actual applicability must be determined portfolio by portfolio. A multi-principal operator may serve products and organisations that follow different legal pathways. The practical answer is not a single blanket rule; it is a policy-controlled platform that can apply the correct approved treatment to each portfolio and retain evidence of what happened.

## 5. Ownership, authority and accountability

The platform must preserve the difference between owning a portfolio, governing collection, performing work, receiving money, verifying payment and making consequential decisions.

| Decision or activity | Proposed accountable party | Delivery or support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Principal relationship and portfolio acceptance | TEKUN Corporation | Portfolio owner consulted; partner supports |
| Collection policy and treatment strategy | TEKUN Corporation within principal authority | Partner configures and operates approved rules |
| Platform build and operation | Partner delivery team | TEKUN Corporation governs priorities and acceptance |
| Daily case work | Authorised collection operation | Platform guides and records work |
| Receipt of customer money | Approved official channel, to be confirmed | Representatives never receive customer money |
| Payment reconciliation | Named finance authority, to be confirmed | Platform imports, matches and exposes exceptions |
| Settlement, write-off and legal action | TEKUN Corporation or portfolio owner as agreed | Operation recommends or escalates only |
| Complaints, dispute and hardship | Named TEKUN/portfolio authority | Specialist operation and platform hold controls |
| Regulatory submission | Accountable entity to be confirmed | Platform supplies controlled evidence and export |

No system permission should imply authority that has not been explicitly granted. Overrides, approvals and policy changes must be attributable, time-stamped and auditable.

## 6. End-to-end collection lifecycle

The solution is a complete operating loop rather than a stand-alone dialler, messaging tool or dashboard.

<!-- diagram: ../tekun-collection-assets/generated-diagrams/diagram-4.svg -->

| Stage | Core purpose | Required evidence or control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intake | Establish an authoritative portfolio population | Source, schema, balance, history, duplication and withdrawal checks |
| Segment | Apply an approved strategy to the correct cohort | Product, arrears, risk, exclusions and strategy version |
| Assign | Give each case a responsible owner | Team, competency, authority, language and workload |
| Notify | Issue required approved communication | Template, version, timing, mandatory information and delivery evidence |
| Verify | Confirm the correct person before disclosure | Verification outcome without unnecessary exposure |
| Engage | Perform controlled contact and record outcome | Time, frequency, channel, script, conduct and complete interaction history |
| Promise | Record a specific commitment accurately | Amount, date, method, tolerance and follow-up |
| Protect | Divert disputes, hardship, vulnerability and legal holds | Immediate hold, specialist owner and release/closure decision |
| Pay | Direct the customer to an approved channel | No agent receipt of funds; official instruction retained |
| Reconcile | Match payment evidence to the correct account | Source evidence, allocation, reversal and unmatched exceptions |
| Cease/close | Stop or adjust treatment when authority changes | Authoritative event, time-to-stop and post-stop exception |
| Report/improve | Turn events into management and principal insight | Governed definitions, portfolio segregation and approved learning |

## 7. Platform solution in plain language

The platform is organised into five connected layers:

1. **Experience layer** — the right workspace for agents, supervisors, management, compliance, finance and each principal.
2. **Authoritative portfolio layer** — principals, portfolios, customers/accounts, cases, agreements, balances and policy versions.
3. **Controlled interaction layer** — queues, notices, identity steps, contacts, promises, complaints and holds.
4. **Evidence and money layer** — payment evidence, reconciliation, reversals, cessation and outcome history.
5. **Enterprise intelligence layer** — service health, performance, customer treatment, risk, financial contribution and roadmap insight.

<!-- diagram: ../tekun-collection-assets/generated-diagrams/diagram-6.svg -->

Each principal should see only its authorised portfolio. TEKUN Corporation retains the governed enterprise view across principals, policies, service health and strategic outcomes.

<!-- pagebreak -->

## 8. Role-based workspaces

### Agent workspace

An authorised representative sees prioritised cases, verified context, the permitted next action, approved communication, identity requirements, contact history, promises and escalation routes. The system blocks or redirects actions that are outside policy.

### Supervisor workspace

A supervisor sees workload, ageing, unreachable accounts, promises due, quality results, exceptions, complaints, holds, staffing and capacity. Supervisors can coach and rebalance work within approved authority, but cannot silently override customer-treatment controls.

### Management and principal views

Management sees outcomes, cost, service health, risks and improvement. Each principal sees its own accepted definitions and portfolio evidence. Compliance and finance receive independent exception views appropriate to their responsibilities.

## 9. Customer treatment by design

Customer treatment is part of the operating system, not a separate policy document. The proposed design supports:

- identity verification before account discussion;
- approved contact windows, frequency and channels;
- controlled notices, scripts and versions;
- prevention of disclosure to unauthorised third parties;
- immediate holds and specialist routing for disputes, complaints, hardship, vulnerability and legal conditions;
- cessation or treatment change following authoritative settlement or arrangement events;
- an accessible complaint record and resolution trail;
- representative appointment, training and competency evidence;
- human authority for consequential outcomes.

Final controls must be approved against the applicable portfolio, governing agreements and qualified Malaysian legal advice. The platform can enforce and prove an approved rule; it cannot itself decide what the law means.

<!-- pagebreak -->

## 10. Payment evidence and reconciliation

A reported promise or claimed payment is not yet a verified collection result. The platform should distinguish:

- promise made;
- payment reported by a customer;
- payment evidence received;
- payment matched and reconciled;
- reversal or adjustment;
- case treatment changed or ceased.

Representatives direct customers to approved payment routes and do not receive money. The final payment recipient, receipt issuer, evidence source, reconciliation owner and service timing must be agreed during discovery.

<!-- diagram: ../tekun-collection-assets/generated-diagrams/diagram-8.svg -->

Financial reporting and any future commercial fee logic should use agreed reconciled evidence, not agent-entered outcomes alone.

## 11. Data, integration, security and resilience

The platform will handle sensitive identity, financial and communication information. Trust therefore depends on explicit data purpose, source, access, change history and recovery.

| Control family | Proposed approach |
| --- | --- |
| Identity and access | Strong authentication, least privilege and principal/role boundaries |
| Data authority | Named source systems, validated interfaces and immutable provenance |
| Sensitive-data protection | Encryption, minimisation, controlled download/export and endpoint policy |
| Audit | Tamper-evident history for material actions, approvals and changes |
| Integration safety | Schema validation, duplicate/reversal handling, retry and exception visibility |
| Monitoring | Availability, errors, feed failures, queue health and unusual access |
| Incident response | Severity, ownership, containment, communication and learning |
| Backup and recovery | Protected backups, recovery objectives and tested restoration |
| Secure change | Review, testing, controlled release, rollback and post-release monitoring |

Privacy roles—controller, processor, sub-processor or other applicable role—are a discovery and contracting outcome. The technical design should follow the confirmed data-flow and responsibility map.

## 12. Reporting and explainable assistance

Different stakeholders need different views of the same governed truth:

- agents need today's safe actions;
- supervisors need workload, quality and exceptions;
- finance needs payment and reconciliation evidence;
- compliance needs conduct, complaints, holds and access events;
- management needs portfolio, cost, service and platform health;
- each principal needs only its own accepted portfolio results;
- TEKUN Corporation governance needs value, risk, growth and roadmap insight.

Advanced assistance should progress from descriptive reporting to diagnostic insight, then guided work and only later predictive or assisted capabilities. Any AI-supported summary, quality sampling or recommendation must remain explainable, auditable and subject to human authority.

## 14. Training, competency and quality

The representative lifecycle is: screen and verify → train → assess → authorise → observe and sample → coach or remediate → reassess. A material concern may suspend or revoke authorisation.

Training covers portfolio policy, identity, confidential communication, permitted contact, notices, payment routes, promises, disputes, complaints, hardship, prohibited conduct, evidence quality, privacy, security and escalation.

Quality scoring should balance outcome, accuracy, conduct, documentation and customer treatment. Recovery alone is not a complete measure of quality.

## 18. Balanced scorecard

“Collection rate” is not complete until the parties agree its numerator, denominator, population, period, exclusions and source.

| Lens | Illustrative governed indicators |
| --- | --- |
| Operations | Time to first action, contact coverage, right-party contact, promises and cases per agent |
| Financial | Reconciled amount, collection/recovery rate, cost-to-collect and net recovery |
| Portfolio | Ageing, roll rate, cure rate and write-off context |

| Platform health | Availability, incidents, restore evidence, failed feeds and reconciliation age |
| Strategic growth | Active principals, onboarding time, capacity headroom and contribution |

The discovery team should produce a KPI dictionary naming formula, source, owner, frequency, exclusions and misinterpretation risk.

## 19. Bounded pilot

The pilot should cover one principal, product or arrears cohort that is large enough to measure and bounded enough to govern.

| Pilot element | Proposed rule |
| --- | --- |
| Baseline | Written comparable history, quality limits and known changes |
| Cohort | Frozen or controlled population with clear inclusions/exclusions |
| Authority | Approved policy, representatives, channels and escalation |
| Technical scope | Minimum complete lifecycle, evidence, reporting and monitoring |
| Evaluation | Operations, financial outcomes, customer treatment, controls and platform health |
| Governance | Weekly operating review and monthly decision forum |
| Gate | Expand, adjust, re-baseline or stop based on agreed evidence |

The pilot succeeds only when performance, conduct, reconciliation and platform-health evidence are acceptable together. No result should be presented without its cohort and definition.

## 20. Commercial principles

Pricing cannot be responsibly set before portfolio volume, channels, service hours, staffing, integrations, risk allocation and performance definitions are known.

The recommended commercial design principles are:

- separate one-time mobilisation from recurring platform and service cost;
- connect any outcome-based element only to reconciled qualifying evidence;
- avoid incentives that reward recovery while weakening customer treatment;
- define pass-through communication or integration costs transparently;
- use service credits or remedies only against agreed measurable obligations;
- preserve export and transition support;
- review economics after a controlled pilot rather than pricing from assumptions.

No commercial archetype is selected in this proposal.

## 22. Long-term partnership value, assurance and transition readiness

Value deepens through four layers:

1. **Foundation value** — a governed platform, baseline and control model.
2. **Operating value** — trained teams, reliable service, integrations and evidence.
3. **Growth value** — faster onboarding, more principals and accepted reporting.
4. **Compounding value** — portfolio knowledge, trusted governance and a jointly governed roadmap.

TEKUN Corporation should retain complete export, configuration, operating records and transition support from day one. This protects institutional control while giving the operating partner a reason to remain useful through expertise and continuing improvement.

### Assurance evidence

| Assurance question | Evidence expected |
| --- | --- |
| Are only authorised people acting? | Identity, role, competency and authorisation records |
| Are actions permitted now? | Notice, contact, consent, hold and authority-gate evidence |
| Was the customer treated fairly? | Interaction history, quality sample and protected-case record |
| Is a collection result real? | Reconciled payment evidence and governed cohort definition |
| Did payment stop action correctly? | Cessation timestamp and post-stop exception report |
| Is each principal separated? | Access/isolation tests and principal-scoped reporting |
| Can an incident be understood and service recovered? | Monitoring, audit, incident, backup and restore evidence |
| Can TEKUN Corporation govern or transition? | Documentation, export and tested transition exercise |

### Transition-ready asset inventory

From mobilisation onward, the governed asset set should include usable data export; configuration and policy versions; a data dictionary; interface specifications; operating and incident runbooks; representative and competency records; open complaint, hardship, dispute and legal-hold registers; permitted interaction and reconciliation history; change and release logs; access/isolation evidence; backup and restoration proof; cessation evidence; and a tested transition plan with named owners.

## 23. Discovery agenda

Before an implementation or commercial commitment, the joint team should confirm:

- legal entity, portfolio and principal relationships;
- current service providers, systems, processes and pain points;
- authorisation or registration status relevant to each portfolio;
- account volumes, products, arrears, channels and historical outcomes;
- policy, notices, scripts, settlements, complaints and hardship pathways;
- customer payment route, reconciliation and fee evidence;
- data sources, roles, retention, security and integration constraints;
- workforce model, hours, languages, competency and supervision;
- baseline, KPI dictionary, pilot cohort and guardrails;
- procurement, commercial inputs, transition expectations and decision process.

These questions are not weaknesses. They are the information required to produce a responsible design and a fair business case.
