# Claude Prototype — Design Notes

Three independent slides built to explore meaningful visual density, real
evidence, and asymmetric composition for the Sifututor ecosystem executive
deck. Content is grounded in the governed sourcebook and mirrors the approved
copy already used for storyboard slides 05, 08 and 14 in
`sifututor-ecosystem-executive-presentation.html`; only the visual treatment
is a fresh exploration.

## Shared system

- **Canvas and palette**: cream stage (`#f3f1e8`) with navy `#111842`, blue
  `#2237b8`, orange `#f45124`, cyan `#53d5e5` and peach `#ffd8cb` as the
  working set, matching the approved reference-style system.
- **Type**: locally bundled Manrope variable font only. Headlines sit at 45px
  with tight tracking; body copy stays at 16.5–17px. No uppercase editorial
  shouting — restrained scale throughout, per the brief.
- **Content field**: every slide reserves the same band (x:64–1856,
  y:300–992) for its main composition, so the three slides read as one
  family despite very different internal shapes.
- **Evidence discipline**: every image is either an actual product
  screenshot (Ripple Suite, Creative Hub), a labelled prototype (Learnest,
  tagged `Prototype` wherever it appears), labelled generated/illustrative
  photography, or a genuine diagram/map. Nothing simulates a screenshot for a
  system that has no approved capture (SIMS, Parent + Tutor, Kelasapp,
  Finch, Analytics + Agent OS) — those are represented through a hub
  diagram, an icon glyph, or real human photography, each labelled
  honestly rather than faked as UI evidence.
- **Screenshot legibility rule**: any card built from a real interface
  screenshot uses a caption bar placed *outside* the image (not an overlay),
  so the evidence itself is never obscured by its own label. Overlay chips
  are reserved for the two generated photographs, which have enough
  negative space and a gradient scrim to keep white text legible.

## Slide 1 — "One learning need becomes a managed end-to-end service"

**Silhouette**: a large radial process ring (58% of the content field) paired
with a stacked evidence column on the right (42%).

The ring is the dominant visual anchor: five arcs (Demand, Match, Deliver,
Money, Support) run clockwise from the top, each connected by a short leader
line and dot to a small annotation card outside the ring — the "small
connected annotations" pattern applied literally, so a reader can trace every
stage from its arc to its description without a legend. The colour of each
arc, its leader line and its annotation dot are the same hue, so the ring
reads as one coherent system rather than five unrelated fragments.

The right column turns three of those five stages into evidence rather than
just claims: an actual Ripple Suite screenshot (cropped to keep the Accounts
/ Reconciliation / Tutor Payments module row legible) proves stage 04
("controlled money movement"), and two illustrative photographs — clearly
tagged — stand in for the human moments in stages 01–02 and 05, since no
interface evidence exists for a customer-facing "demand" or "support" screen
in the current asset set. The right column explains the ring; it does not
float independently of it.

## Slide 2 — "Portfolio breadth comes from different systems doing different jobs"

**Silhouette**: a hub-and-spoke diagram (57.5%) rather than the ring from
slide 1, deliberately avoiding a repeated shape — plus a mosaic of three
zoom-in evidence cards on the right (42.5%), split into one wide top card and
two narrower cards below it.

SIMS sits as a solid navy hub at the centre, carrying its own governed status
(`Live · May 2025`, reused verbatim from the approved deck rather than
invented). Five dashed spokes reach exactly the five categories named in the
content contract: Ripple Suite, Parent + Tutor, Finch + Creative Hub,
Learnest + Kelasapp, and Analytics + Agent OS. Each spoke node carries real
evidence at thumbnail scale — a Ripple screenshot, a Creative Hub screenshot,
a Learnest screenshot (with its own micro "Prototype" dot), an illustrative
human photograph for the stakeholder-channel node, and a small hand-drawn
data-visualisation glyph (not a fabricated product screenshot) for Analytics
+ Agent OS, since no approved capture exists for that system.

The right-hand cards zoom into three of the five nodes at legible size —
Ripple, Creative Hub, Learnest — so the density in the hub diagram is
explained rather than left as decoration. Parent + Tutor and Analytics +
Agent OS are represented honestly in the hub without a matching zoom card,
because no approved screenshot evidence exists to zoom into.

## Slide 3 — "Consolidate the operating spine, then multiply its value"

**Silhouette**: an ascending staircase list on the left (35%) and a single
connected spine-to-map diagram on the right (63%) — the widest dominant
anchor of the three, justified because horizon 4 and horizon 5 are one
continuous idea (the spine multiplying, then adapting geographically) rather
than five independent tiles.

The five horizon cards step diagonally down-right (increasing left margin
per card), giving the list a physical sense of ascent instead of a flat
list — each keeps its governed capability items and its "Gate" qualifier, so
horizons read as earned states, not a scheduled timeline.

The right panel is deliberately not a generic chart: a "Reusable operating
spine" node fans out — via dashed connectors, not a decorative flourish — to
the four named product examples from the content contract (Learnest,
Kelasapp, Finch, Nakngaji), explicitly captioned as illustrative reuse and
not a committed roadmap. Below it, the actual Malaysia outline map (the same
approved public-domain asset used elsewhere in the deck) anchors horizon 5,
paired with the four localisation factors named in the contract — rules,
language, payments, support — as a small connected grid rather than loose
text. The slide's source note repeats the contract's own guardrail almost
verbatim: horizons are capability gates, not launch dates or public
commitments.

## Honest limitations

- No approved screenshot evidence exists for SIMS, the Parent app, the Tutor
  app, Kelasapp, Finch, or Agent OS individually. Those systems are shown
  through diagrams, icon glyphs or labelled illustrative photography instead
  of simulated interfaces — this keeps every visual claim traceable, but it
  does mean the deck's most technically "authoritative" system (SIMS) has no
  UI evidence of its own, only its governed status label.
- The two generated photographs (parent coordination, operations
  coordinator) are reused across both slide 1 and slide 2 in different crops
  — there is no second, distinct piece of illustrative photography approved
  in the asset set for a third context.
- The Ripple Suite screenshot is reused three times (once per slide 1 and
  twice across slide 2's hub thumbnail and zoom card) with different crops.
  This is a real, single approved screenshot, not three different captures.
